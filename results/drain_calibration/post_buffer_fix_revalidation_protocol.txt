AssuredQoS Post-Buffer-Fix Drain Revalidation Protocol
=====================================================

Purpose
-------
Revalidate the previously selected 1000-ms drain duration after
correcting the per-UE bearer-buffer admission rule.

The simulator previously admitted an incoming packet whenever the
existing bearer occupancy was below the configured per-UE capacity.
This could allow the incoming packet to push bearer occupancy above
the configured 81920-byte limit.

The corrected admission invariant is:

    current_bearer_bytes + incoming_packet_bytes <= 81920

Because this correction changes packet admission, packet drops,
bearer occupancy, and potentially end-of-run residual behavior,
the pre-fix drain calibration cannot by itself establish that the
previously selected 1000-ms drain remains appropriate.

Drain semantics
---------------
Traffic generation stops at the end of the fixed 10000-ms active
period.

During the drain period:

    no new traffic is generated;
    scheduling continues;
    retransmission processing continues;
    transport-block completion continues;
    packet completion accounting continues.

Active-period throughput remains based only on bytes delivered during
the active period divided by the fixed active duration.

Revalidation metrics
--------------------
Use the same drain-stability metrics and definitions as the frozen
pre-fix drain-calibration methodology.

For each scenario, seed, and slice, compare the relevant residual
fraction and p99 delay quantities between drain durations.

Residual absolute stability tolerance:

    0.005

p99 stability tolerance:

    max(
        0.1 ms,
        0.02 * reference_p99
    )

No tolerance may be relaxed after observing post-fix results.

Decision rule
-------------
The 1000-ms drain may be retained only if, for every tested traffic
scenario, seed, and slice, the 1000-ms result is stable relative to
the 2000-ms result under the frozen tolerances.

The 500-ms result is included to test whether the corrected simulator
has materially reduced the necessary drain duration.

Case A:
    500 ms is not stable relative to the larger tested drains,
    while 1000 ms is stable relative to 2000 ms.

Decision:
    Retain 1000 ms.

Case B:
    500 ms is also stable relative to every larger tested drain.

Decision:
    Do not automatically select 500 ms and do not retain 1000 ms
    merely because it was selected before the buffer correction.
    Reopen the lower-drain search prospectively on the corrected
    simulator to determine the smallest stable duration.

Case C:
    1000 ms is not stable relative to 2000 ms.

Decision:
    The previous 1000-ms selection is invalid under the corrected
    simulator. Define and freeze a prospective expanded drain search
    before collecting additional drain durations.

Case D:
    Any run violates packet-accounting conservation, produces an
    invalid metric, or violates the corrected bearer-capacity
    invariant.

Decision:
    Stop. Do not perform drain selection until the correctness issue
    is resolved.

Interpretation
--------------
This is a targeted semantic-change revalidation, not a claim that the
original drain experiment was incorrectly executed.

The original drain experiment characterized the simulator semantics
that existed at that time.

The post-fix experiment determines whether its selected drain duration
remains valid after enforcing the intended bearer-buffer capacity.
